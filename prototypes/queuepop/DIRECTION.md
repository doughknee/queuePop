# QueuePop / one home, three worlds

Scope: standalone, interactive visual concept. No production UI, Python, game client, live statistics, notifications, or backend changes.

Shared palette: charcoal #141618, warm white #EEEAE1, muted gold #D3B981, grey #A5A6A6, quiet sage #A7BCAB. Retain the original Queue Sigil; Segoe UI provides familiar desktop controls. Shared top bar, game links, companion, saved plans, focus styles, and confirmation language.

League: midnight blue #081A24, luminous blue #93D8E5, antique gold #C8AA6E. Cinzel for ceremonial headings, Marcellus for softer editorial moments, Segoe UI for practical detail. Sweeping champion splash, a large left-aligned title, restrained inlaid rules, meaningful champion selection. Interior pages retain art beside legible planning information.

TFT: plum #211D36, violet #B9A0ED, honey #F5CD74, seafoam #9BD6C0. Rounded, heavy Trebuchet headings. A friendly illustrated world and a tactile hexagonal team board, compact bench, and readable side notes. Deliberately distinct from League's ceremonial portrait.

Composition:
```
Shared:  QueuePop | Home · League · TFT · Deadlock | Companion
Home:    greeting + saved plan / three generous illustrated world entrances
League:  game navigation / cinematic champion + left title / plan + recent match
TFT:     game navigation / playful landscape / composition board + strategy notes
```

Review before implementation: a second stat dashboard would repeat the rejected first Deadlock pass. Prefer game artwork, character choice, and playable planning tools; statistics support the task. Shared brand is intentionally quiet so the game worlds can be distinct. No popup menus or modals. Deadlock remains approved and intact; a persistent isolated game view reuses its original local scripts/styles/assets under the shared masthead.

Fixture data is illustrative and intentionally not represented as current patch advice. TFT formations use familiar champion portraits to demonstrate the planner, not a claimed live set roster.

## Interior depth

League's playbook uses a full-height champion banner beside a restrained illuminated leaf. Purchases sit in circular relic settings along a three-part path; the selected item explains a decision in place. The journal gives each match an illustrated chapter, while reports pair a cinematic outcome with a short, selectable narrative. Keep gold as structure, blue as atmosphere, and typography readable rather than ornamental everywhere.

TFT's interiors use collectible formation studies, generous rounded corners, gold carry tokens, and a violet strategy table. Warm paper notes hold one decision at a time. Match reports center the board itself, with read-only snapshots and a clear path back to the editable plan. Do not imply these illustrative rosters correspond to a live set.

## League world pass

Design tokens: obsidian `#050D14`, deep blue `#0D1C25`, aged gold `#C8AA6E`, ivory `#F0E6D0`, spell light `#79C9CF`, muted defeat `#9F6058`. Cinzel carries champion names; Marcellus provides editorial hierarchy; Segoe UI keeps controls readable.

The old overview led with a generic welcome headline and put the champion behind it. The new composition gives the champion the scene: large identity at left, uninterrupted character artwork at right, a compact portrait selector, and a quiet shared QueuePop header. Gold framing, clipped corners, and concentric engraving borrow the material vocabulary of the League client without borrowing Deadlock's paper or grunge.

```
Shared QueuePop masthead / League navigation
Champion scene: identity + action | character artwork | portrait selection
Preparation gateway             | most relevant sample match
One selected focus for the next game
```

The collection opens dedicated champion dossiers with real ability artwork, a selectable spellbook, strengths, considerations, and a locally remembered focus. Practical interiors inherit the same framing and type, while retaining their functioning item choices and match chapters. Match reports show an explicitly illustrative final loadout. Keep focus, ability selection, and navigation inline; no popup menus.

Review criterion: more gold around the old boxes would not meet the brief. Composition and content both changed. Do not extend this League pass into TFT or the approved Deadlock design.

## Wide displays and navigation

Use a full-window scene with a composed content area, following the spatial principle of the approved Deadlock concept. Do not cap the entire League canvas: cap reading and interaction areas instead. On wide displays, give champion artwork room to breathe while preserving a comfortable distance between related controls.

The League navigation is its own landmark: crest at left, four illustrated destinations in the center, selected champion at right. Its gold cut-corner active plate and inset diamond provide a stronger sense of place. Tablet and phone layouts retain direct access to every destination without a popup menu. This visual language belongs to League; the shared QueuePop masthead and other game worlds remain separate.

## Armory and chronicle

Give each champion a personal field book: Ahri's second opening, Lux's line of light, Yasuo's measured storm. The identity portrait and saved plan anchor the page; purchases become inspectable objects in engraved settings, with a visible reason and ability connection. The three numerals represent the actual opening, developing, and adapting sequence.

Make match history feel collected rather than tabulated. One tall illustrated cover leads, with two supporting memories; outcome, champion, duration and KDA remain easy to scan. In a report, the chapter controls connect directly to an illustrated battlefield sketch and a short decision narrative. Keep the sketches deliberately schematic and label them as illustrative, not replays. No modal flows, speculative live statistics, or new motion are needed.

The armory's material pass removes the repeated item plaques. Purchases sit in open engraved settings within a painted sanctuary; selection changes the light and ring emphasis. A pale, irregular field note gives the reasoning a different material and a clear reading surface. The portrait has an arched frame, a worn lower edge, a personal line and a champion motif. Use weathered stone, aged bronze and painted depth to provide character; keep the actual labels and controls restrained.
